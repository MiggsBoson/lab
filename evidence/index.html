    The case

    At 08:00 on a Saturday a jeweller is found dead in his shop. The back window has been smashed from outside. Nothing was taken from the safe.

    A man is questioned two days later. He says he came to the shop at 21:00 on Friday to sell a ring, that the jeweller was alive when he left by the front door at 21:30, and that he never went near the back of the building. Two glass fragments are found on his jacket. Blood on the broken window frame gives a partial DNA profile that matches him.

    Your job is the forensic scientist's: weigh each item of evidence on its own terms, then see what they say together. The court decides guilt. You report how strongly the findings support one proposition over another.

    All people, places and measurements are invented. Transfer rates for glass are Evett's (1986) published estimates; the DNA allele frequencies are made up for practice.

            Time since death

            Body temperature falls in a predictable way after death, so one reading gives an estimate of when death occurred. Every input uncertainty widens that estimate, and the shape of the cooling curve stretches some of them far more than others (the Jacobian).

            Competing explanations

            When two explanations both account for what happened, the evidence cannot separate them (LR = 1). What matters is how probable each explanation is in the first place, and whether a second event depends on the first.

              Case summary

              Combine items with Bayes' rule: posterior odds = prior odds × LR₁ × LR₂ × …. On the log scale each item adds its weight in bans to the starting log-odds. Learn: combining evidence, the prior.

              Multiplying is only valid when items are independent given each proposition. A glass refractive index and the number of fragments, for example, should be combined through the transfer model, not multiplied twice. A likelihood ratio says how much the evidence shifts the odds. It does not say how probable guilt is without the prior.
